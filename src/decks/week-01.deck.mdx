# How Should a Picture Book Behave?

### Week 1 — Picture Books That Misbehave

---

{/* _class: quote */}

"What happens when a picture book refuses to behave like a normal book?"

---

## The core rule

A picture book should make itself easy to read.

- guide your eye
- tell you where to look first
- never leave you unsure what happens next

---

## Where the rules hide (1)

- gaze and reading order
- left page versus right page
- words and pictures
- page boundaries

---

## Where the rules hide (2)

- page turns
- the narrator
- reader expectations

---

## Gaze and reading order

Most readers scan left to right, top to bottom. On a spread, the left page
is usually read before the right — before a single word is read.

---

## Words and pictures

Text and image are expected to agree. If the words say a dog barks, the
picture shows a barking dog.

---

## Page turns

A page turn is a promise. Something changes underneath it — a reveal held
back, a beat of time passing, a joke delayed one breath.

---

## Today: Rule Hunt

- choose a familiar picture book
- find five implicit rules it follows
- pick one
- make one rough alternative spread showing what happens when that rule
  disappears

---

## This Week's Misbehaviour — Find a Rule

- notice one usually invisible convention
- record it in a sentence
- briefly test what changes when you interrupt it
- 15–30 minutes, rough is fine

---

## Why bother noticing?

You cannot deliberately break a rule you have not noticed yet. Everything
the rest of this course asks you to do starts here.
