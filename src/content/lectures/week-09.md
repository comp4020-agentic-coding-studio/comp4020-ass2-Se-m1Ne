Last week gave the reader some say over which order they read in. Week 9
asks for something stronger: an action the reader performs that the story
actually needs, so that if they do nothing, something in the story fails to
happen.

The idea holds together in four parts: **Invitation → Action →
Consequence → Meaning**.

- **Invitation** — the work has to communicate that the reader can or should
  do something, through words, images, spatial arrangement, a character, or
  an obvious physical affordance. The reader shouldn't have to stumble onto
  the interaction through unexplained trial and error.
- **Action** — the reader performs something simple: touch, cover, uncover,
  rotate, move, point, fold, open, close, hold, compare, speak, or another
  ordinary physical action. This doesn't need digital technology, and no
  single mechanism is required.
- **Consequence** — the story has to respond to that action. Ask what
  happens because the reader did it. If they perform the action and nothing
  in the story's meaning changes, the interaction is probably decorative.
- **Meaning** — the interaction should support the story, a character,
  tension, humour, or another intentional effect — not exist purely because
  interacting feels novel.

Every physical book already asks the reader to do something — at minimum,
turn the page. That alone doesn't satisfy Week 9. The difference is whether
the action is framed inside the story's own logic. Ordinary reading: the
reader turns the page because that's how reading continues. Week 9: the
story treats the reader's action as causing, preventing, revealing,
changing, helping, or interrupting an event.

This is also where the main risk sits: interaction is not automatically
meaningful participation. A gimmick asks the reader to do something because
it's fun or unusual; it doesn't change what the story means. A simple test
catches this — remove the reader's action entirely and ask what the story
loses. If the answer is almost nothing, the interaction needs revision. It's
worth asking why this action, why at this moment, and what consequence
actually follows from it.

Today's studio, Nothing Happens Without You, asks you to design one story
event that genuinely can't happen until a reader performs one action, and
test whether another reader actually understands what they're being asked to
do and why it matters.
