A picture book asks you to trust the picture: this is what is there, drawn or
photographed as it happened. But a picture never shows everything — it shows
what someone chose to include, from where, at what distance, and with how
much space left empty around it. Change any of those choices and the same
subject can tell a different story, even though nothing in front of the lens
or the pen actually moved.

This week works through six choices every image makes, whether or not the
reader notices them:

- **Crop** — what falls outside the frame can change how you read what's left
  inside it. A missing detail is not always innocent.
- **Frame** — the boundary marks what counts as the event. Everything inside
  it feels like "what happened"; everything outside it disappears.
- **Scale** — making something larger or smaller in the frame shifts its
  importance, its vulnerability, or its distance from the reader.
- **Perspective** — where the reader seems to stand changes their
  relationship to the subject: looking down, looking up, held close, or held
  back.
- **Orientation** — turning or reorienting an image can unsettle assumptions
  about which way is stable, or which way it should be read.
- **Negative space** — empty space around a subject is still a decision. It
  can suggest isolation, anticipation, or that something is missing.

None of this is about drawing skill or camera settings — it's about the
decisions that shape any image, however it's made. Today's studio,
Same Picture, Different Truth, asks you to take one source image and reframe
it four different ways, so you see these choices change meaning on your own
material instead of taking it on faith.
