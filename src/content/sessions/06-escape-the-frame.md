## Before the session

Bring simple materials for a short three- or four-unit sequence — paper,
pens, scissors and old magazines, or your phone. You're using the shape of
the page itself, so keep the drawing rough and the idea clear.

## Escape the Frame

This is the main studio activity. Build a short sequence — three or four
page/spread-like units — where a character, object, word, or other element
interacts with a page boundary. Choose **one** primary boundary for this
exercise:

- edge;
- gutter;
- margin;
- text block.

Suggested process:

1. Establish where the story normally appears to exist on the page.
2. Choose your one boundary.
3. Give that boundary a clear role — what does it normally hold in, or keep
   out?
4. Make something approach, resist, cross, escape, enter, or otherwise
   interact with it.
5. Show a consequence of that interaction.

Step 5 matters most. Crossing the boundary should change something — what
can be seen, where something can go, the relationship between characters, the
order information is encountered in, or the reader's understanding of the
page. Trying to use every boundary at once usually weakens all of them; stay
with the one you chose.

None of this needs polished illustration. Rough drawing, collage,
photography, typography, or simple shapes are all legitimate.

## Studio check

Once you have your sequence, answer briefly:

1. What was the normal spatial rule?
2. Which boundary became part of the story?
3. What broke the rule?
4. What changed because of the break?

If your answer to question 4 is effectively "nothing," go back and revise the
sequence rather than keeping the break as decoration.

## This Week's Misbehaviour — Use the Edge

A separate 15–30 minute experiment from Escape the Frame:

- make one page or spread where the outer edge is necessary to understanding
  the event, not just a boundary around it;
- test it against one question: if the page had no edge, would this idea
  still work?
- briefly state what the edge does, and why the idea depends on it.

This is one page built around the edge specifically, not a smaller version of
the full sequence — keep it quick and rough.

## Afterwards

Add your sequence and your Use the Edge note to your Misbehaviour Toolbox.
Week 5 asked where a reader looks on the page. Week 6 asked what the page
itself — its edge, its gutter, its margin, its text block — can do once it
stops being treated as neutral space. Next week looks at what happens between
one page and the next, when turning the page becomes something the story can
use.
