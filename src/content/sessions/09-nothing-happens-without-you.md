## Before the session

Bring simple materials for a short three- to five-unit prototype — paper,
pens, scissors, cards, or your phone. You're designing one action the reader
has to take, not a fully built interactive object.

## Nothing Happens Without You

This is the main studio activity. Build a short sequence or prototype — three
to five page/spread-like states or units — where one important story event
cannot occur without a reader action. It should move through these
conceptual stages, which don't need to be on separate pages:

1. **Situation** — something is happening, missing, blocked, waiting, or
   unresolved.
2. **Invitation** — the reader is given a clear reason or cue to act.
3. **Action** — the reader performs one simple action.
4. **Consequence** — something changes because of that action.
5. **After-effect** — it's clear why that change matters to the story.

Choose **one** primary reader action rather than stacking several unrelated
interactions. It should be simple enough to understand, possible with your
chosen materials, and directly connected to its consequence.

None of this needs polished engineering. Paper, rough drawing, collage,
cards, cut-outs, typography, or simple digital mock-ups are all legitimate —
the focus is the relationship between the reader's action and the story, not
the build quality.

## Test it

Give your prototype to another person without explaining what they're
supposed to do first. Observe:

1. Do they notice the invitation?
2. What do they think they're supposed to do?
3. Do they perform the intended action?
4. Do they understand what changed because of it?
5. Does the consequence feel connected to their action?

If they can't discover the interaction at all, revise the invitation. If
they perform it but see no meaningful consequence, revise the story
relationship instead. Keep this light and practical.

## Studio check

After testing, briefly answer:

1. What does the reader do?
2. What happens because they do it?
3. What would happen if they did nothing?
4. Why does this story need the reader's action?

## This Week's Misbehaviour — Make Me Do Something

A separate 15–30 minute experiment from Nothing Happens Without You — not
another complete prototype.

Make one quick page, spread, or object that silently encourages another
person to perform one simple action, using visual or spatial design rather
than a written instruction. Show it to someone without explanation, then
record:

1. what you wanted them to do;
2. what they actually did;
3. what visual or spatial cue they responded to.

This is about testing invitation and affordance quickly — it doesn't need
the full narrative consequence the main studio asks for.

## Afterwards

Add your prototype and your Make Me Do Something notes to your Misbehaviour
Toolbox. Week 8 asked which order a reader would choose to read in. Week 9
asked what happens because a reader acted — turning their action into
something the story's cause-and-effect actually depends on. Next week looks
at what happens when a character pushes back against what the story, the
narrator, or the reader expects of them.
