Pick one convention picture books normally follow, and break only that one.
The constraint is the point: a single, legible break is easier to test,
easier to defend, and easier to tell apart from a mistake than several
changes made at once.

## The brief

> Find a rule a picture book normally obeys, and break it on purpose.

The rule can be visual, textual, spatial, or narrative. What matters is that
the break is deliberate and that it does something specific to how the page
is read, not that it looks unusual.

## What you submit

A prototype of roughly 4–6 pages or spread-equivalents, in whatever medium
suits the rule you are breaking — drawn, collaged, photographed, typeset, or
built from found material. Alongside it, a short explanation of roughly
150–250 words covering the rule, how you broke it, and the reader effect you
intended.
