A book that gives up something different the second time it is read. This is
not only a plot twist: background narrative, visual clues, reading order,
unreliable narration, page relationships, reader action, perspective, or
withheld information can all do the work.

## The brief

> Make a short book that reads differently the second time through, and find
> out whether it actually does.

Decide what changes on rereading and how a reader is meant to notice it, then
test that decision against readers who do not yet know the trick.

## What you submit

A prototype of roughly 6–10 pages or spread-equivalents, tested with about
2–3 readers without the trick explained in advance. Keep lightweight evidence
of what each reader noticed, missed, misunderstood, or reconsidered — notes,
recordings, or annotated pages are all fine. Alongside the prototype, a design
response of roughly 300 words setting out what you expected readers to
experience against what actually happened, and what you changed as a result.
